Case one Product vision & experience design 2015 – 2017

uGrow

Philips Avent — Amsterdam & Bangalore

Philips Avent was a traditional analog baby products company: bottles, soothers, breast pumps, baby monitors. No digital proposition, no platform, no ongoing relationship with parents after the purchase.

Through a series of 'Smart Parenting' discovery sprints, a cross-functional team of business, research and design identified the opportunity for a suite of connected products built around a parenting platform. I led the experience design throughout, from early discovery to delivery, shaping the product vision and end-to-end user experience while researchers validated with mothers and the business built the case.

The vision was funded and the result was uGrow: a core app, connected baby monitor, smart thermometer and smart bottle sleeve, the first medically certified smart parenting platform in both the US and EU markets.

Outcome

Shipped and certified in the US and EU. Recognized with the CES Innovation Award, iF Product Design Award and Red Dot Award. Proof that a hardware-first company could build a lasting digital relationship with its customers.

Case two Platform strategy & innovation portfolio 2017 – 2020

RIZE

WPG Publishers — Amsterdam

WPG Publishers owned some of the Netherlands' most trusted mindstyle magazine brands, Happinez, Psychologie Magazine, Yoga Magazine, but was facing declining subscriptions and insufficient digital growth. The business model that had worked for decades was no longer enough.

A workshop with the magazines' chief editors mapped the landscape across Mind, Body and Soul and surfaced a choice: Sunset (exit) or Sunrise (survive and grow through transformation). The board funded a three month sprint to build the Sunrise case.

The strategic insight at the core was simple but counterintuitive: publishers sit on the most valuable content asset in the market, but can no longer monetize it directly. Flip the model. Use content as the marketing vehicle and sell products, training and memberships in the wellbeing domain on the back of it. That insight became RIZE, a membership-based wellbeing platform built on WPG's existing brand authority and audience trust.

Over three months a cross-functional team of editorial staff, creatives and innovators developed the full concept, with the vision and strategic direction driving every step. Schwung, WPG's innovation studio, also ran 150 other ideas through a stage-gated process in parallel. Most were invalidated early, by design. The ones that survived launched: Coachfinder grew to steady revenue within 18 months, ThrillerAbo ran successfully for years.

The board declined to fund RIZE, judging the pivot too risky. Within a year the magazine business was sold off in parts, with only core book publishing surviving.

Outcome

Coachfinder and ThrillerAbo launched and generated revenue. RIZE was not funded. The strategic logic it was based on played out exactly as forecast — just without WPG as the one to act on it.

Case three Product concept & experience design 2023 – 2024

YouR

Rabobank — via Macaw, Amsterdam

Rabobank's 50,000 employees navigated a fragmented landscape of legacy HR, IT and Facilities tools with no coherent digital experience. Every secondary task — requesting leave, ordering equipment, finding a policy — required jumping between disconnected systems in a landscape that had grown organically for decades.

As lead at Macaw I led a design team through research, ideation and concept development. We conducted 18 structured interviews, mapped external trends including emerging AI and employee experience platforms, and synthesized findings into a clear design direction.

The result was YouR — a name and concept I originated — a personalized, AI-assisted employee platform that replaces the fragmented tool landscape with a single intelligent interaction layer. The full deliverable included a vision document, interaction model, change framework built on three pillars (Employee Journey Centric, Made to Matter, Cooperative at Scale) and a concept car design validated with Rabobank stakeholders.

Outcome

The concept was handed over as a strategic blueprint for implementation.

Case four Organisational vision & product strategy 2024 – 2026

AirportOS

Amsterdam Airport Schiphol — Amsterdam

Schiphol was operating at the limits of its system. With no buffers left, capacity constraints hitting daily operations, and two departments — APOC and FACT — working in parallel without a shared direction, the airport lacked both the tools and the organisational clarity to make data-driven decisions at scale.

As Strategic Change Manager I developed the product vision and digitalisation strategy for AirportOS — a name and frame I originated — positioning it as the intelligence layer for airport operations: a data platform that gives Schiphol the ability to predict, coordinate and optimise capacity across the entire chain, from strategic planning to real-time operations.

In parallel I led the merger of APOC and FACT into a single department, defining the new operating model, team structure and planning cycle. I also framed the strategic ambition: ACPC as the trusted broker of insights and conductor of the system, working toward becoming the best-performing airport in Europe.

The full deliverable included a shared vision, a capabilities framework, a maturity roadmap across four levels, and governance for the transformation programme through 2030.

Outcome

A strategic blueprint and shared direction handed over to the organisation, with governance and roadmap in place for a multi-year transformation programme.
